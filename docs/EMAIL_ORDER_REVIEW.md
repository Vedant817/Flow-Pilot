# Inbound email order handling

The classifier is an intent hint, **not permission to write**. The Gmail webhook checks every classified new order with `parseNewOrder`. Clear purchase intent with incomplete delivery details becomes an **inbound order draft**, not a fulfillment order. Drafts retain the original email and placement timestamp, tentative product suggestions and optional requested delivery date/time. Neither a missing delivery date nor a missing delivery time is silently fabricated.

An automatically confirmed order requires unambiguous structured product lines **and** an explicit valid delivery date and time. A reviewer may correct draft product suggestions, enter the delivery slot and confirm through `/email-drafts`. Confirmation validates all fields again and atomically creates an order and resolves the draft in a MongoDB transaction; it fails closed without transaction support. Orders created from inbound mail use `date/time` for the **placement timestamp** (compatible with existing date-based analytics) and the separate `requestedDeliveryDate/Time` fields for fulfillment. Existing orders keep their legacy date semantics. Order documents use `gmail:<messageId>` as their unique idempotency key; the existing unique order index prevents a retry from creating another order.

Messages needing human attention become `email_reviews` records, deduplicated by `(orgId, gmailMessageId)`. The authenticated `/email-reviews` page (linked from Errors) shows the original message, reason and candidate changes; it supports resolving with a note, dismissing, or applying a **reviewed** amendment to a specified pending order. The apply action verifies the order's organization and sender, requires a supported explicit operation with a unique exact product match, blocks priced-order quantity changes, and commits the order, review status and rollup together in a MongoDB transaction. It fails closed without transaction support. Date-only changes require an explicit ISO or numeric date; vague terms such as “next Monday” remain for manual handling.

**Local synthetic coverage:** `npx tsx scripts/eval/write-policy.ts` reports **18/20 new-order examples captured as drafts, 2/20 sent to review, 0/20 automatically confirmed**. The classifier-plus-intent flow captures those same 18 (10/12 from the held-out portion) and creates zero drafts from the other 60 examples on this set. Thirteen of the 18 drafts have tentative product suggestions. Five of the 20 update emails yield a supported change proposal. These results are not production accuracy estimates; the 32/48 train/test split was authored locally and reused across development. Improve the parser against separately labeled real mail and rerun the write-policy audit before widening automatic fulfillment.

The review page does not submit arbitrary edits to orders. For unsupported changes, use the existing order-management process, then mark the review resolved with a note. `resolved` and `dismissed` items remain queryable via `/api/email-reviews?status=resolved|dismissed`; the open list is limited to 100 newest items. Drafts have their own `/api/email-drafts?status=draft|confirmed|dismissed` and `/email-drafts` page.
